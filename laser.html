1. Emission and Absorption Processes

Absorption

Absorption occurs when an atom absorbs a photon and moves from a lower energy level E1 to a higher energy level E2.

Energy equation: E1 + hν = E2

Spontaneous Emission

Spontaneous emission occurs when an excited atom returns to a lower energy level on its own and emits a photon.

Energy equation: E2 = E1 + hν

Stimulated Emission

Stimulated emission occurs when an incoming photon causes an excited atom to emit another photon.

Energy equation: E2 = E1 + hν

In stimulated emission, the two photons have the same:

  • Frequency
  • Phase
  • Direction of propagation

2. Einstein A and B Coefficients

Einstein coefficients describe the probabilities of absorption and emission of radiation by atoms.

Einstein A Coefficient

A21 represents the probability of spontaneous emission per unit time.

Einstein B12 Coefficient

B12 represents the probability of absorption due to incident radiation.

Einstein B21 Coefficient

B21 represents the probability of stimulated emission due to incident radiation.

Important Relations

Energy difference: E2 - E1 = hν

For non-degenerate energy levels: B12 = B21

Einstein First relation: B12 = B21

Derivation of Einstein's Second Equation

Consider two energy levels E1 and E2, where E2 > E1.

1. Rate of Absorption:

R12 = N1 B12 ρ(ν)

2. Rate of Spontaneous Emission:

R21 = N2 A21

3. Rate of Stimulated Emission:

R21 = N2 B21 ρ(ν)

At thermal equilibrium:

N1 B12 ρ(ν) = N2 A21 + N2 B21 ρ(ν)

Rearranging:

ρ(ν) = A21 / [B12(N1/N2) − B21]

Using Boltzmann distribution and Planck's radiation law, we obtain:

A21 / B21 = 8πhν³ / c³

Hence proved.

3. Working Principle of Laser

LASER stands for Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation.

A laser works on the principle of stimulated emission. It produces a highly directional, coherent and nearly monochromatic beam of light.

Main Components

  • Active medium
  • Pumping source
  • Optical resonator

Working

  1. Energy is supplied to the active medium.
  2. Atoms are excited to higher energy states.
  3. Population inversion is achieved.
  4. Stimulated emission amplifies the light.
  5. The laser beam emerges through the partially reflecting mirror.

4. Metastable State

A metastable state is an excited energy state in which atoms remain for a comparatively longer time than in ordinary excited states.

It helps atoms accumulate in the excited state and makes population inversion possible.

5. Population Inversion

Population inversion is the condition in which the number of atoms in a higher energy state exceeds the number in the lower energy state.

Condition: N2 > N1

Population inversion is necessary for laser action.

6. Conditions Necessary for Laser Action

  • An active medium must be present.
  • A suitable pumping mechanism is required.
  • Population inversion must be achieved.
  • A metastable state is helpful for storing excited atoms.
  • An optical resonator helps amplify light.

7. Optical Resonator

An optical resonator consists of two mirrors placed around the active medium.

  • One mirror is highly reflecting.
  • The other mirror is partially reflecting.
  • Light reflects repeatedly through the active medium.
  • A portion of the light exits through the partially reflecting mirror.

8. Ruby Laser

A ruby laser is a solid-state, three-level laser. Its active medium is a ruby crystal containing chromium ions.

Main Components

  • Ruby rod
  • Xenon flash lamp
  • Two mirrors forming an optical resonator

Working

  1. The flash lamp supplies energy to the ruby crystal.
  2. Chromium ions become excited.
  3. Atoms accumulate in a metastable state.
  4. Stimulated emission produces laser light.

The ruby laser emits red light of approximately 694.3 nm wavelength.

9. He-Ne Laser

A helium-neon laser is a gas laser. Its active medium is a mixture of helium and neon gases.

Main Components

  • Gas discharge tube
  • Helium and neon gas mixture
  • Electrodes
  • Optical resonator

Working

  1. An electric discharge excites helium atoms.
  2. Energy is transferred from helium atoms to neon atoms.
  3. Population inversion is established in neon.
  4. Stimulated emission produces laser light.

A common He-Ne laser emits red light at approximately 632.8 nm.

10. Semiconductor Laser

A semiconductor laser uses a semiconductor material as its active medium.

It commonly uses a forward-biased p-n junction. Electron-hole recombination can produce photons, and stimulated emission amplifies the light.

Applications

  • Optical fibre communication
  • Barcode scanners
  • Laser printers
  • Optical storage devices

11. Applications of Laser

  • Medical surgery
  • Optical fibre communication
  • Industrial cutting and welding
  • Scientific research
  • Distance measurement
  • Barcode scanning

12. Numerical Problems

Photon energy can be calculated using:

E = hν

Since ν = c / λ:

E = hc / λ

Where:

  • E = photon energy in joules
  • h = Planck's constant = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J s
  • ν = frequency in hertz
  • c = speed of light ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s
  • λ = wavelength in metres

    • Solved Numerical

    Question: Calculate the energy of a photon having wavelength 600 nm.

    Formula: E = hc / λ

    Given:

    h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s

    c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

    λ = 600 × 10⁻⁹ m

    Solution:

    E = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸) / (600 × 10⁻⁹)

    Answer: E = 3.313 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Advanced Solved Numericals - Laser

Numerical 1: Photon Energy

Question: A laser emits light of wavelength 632.8 nm. Calculate the energy of one photon in joules and electron volts.

Formula: E = hc / λ

h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s

c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s

λ = 632.8 × 10⁻⁹ m

E = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸) / (632.8 × 10⁻⁹)

E ≈ 3.14 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Since 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J,

Answer: E ≈ 1.96 eV

Numerical 2: Number of Photons

Question: A laser of wavelength 500 nm emits 2 J of energy. Calculate the number of photons emitted.

Formula: N = Etotal / Ephoton

Ephoton = hc / λ

Ephoton = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸) / (500 × 10⁻⁹)

Ephoton = 3.976 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

N = 2 / (3.976 × 10⁻¹⁹)

Answer: N ≈ 5.03 × 10¹⁸ photons

Numerical 3: Laser Power

Question: A laser emits 5 × 10¹⁸ photons per second. If the wavelength is 600 nm, calculate the power of the laser.

Formula: P = N × hc / λ

P = 5 × 10¹⁸ × (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸) / (600 × 10⁻⁹)

Answer: P ≈ 1.66 W

Numerical 4: Frequency and Energy Difference

Question: An electron transition in a laser produces light of wavelength 632.8 nm. Calculate the frequency and energy difference between the two energy levels.

Formula: ν = c / λ

ν = (3 × 10⁸) / (632.8 × 10⁻⁹)

ν ≈ 4.74 × 10¹⁴ Hz

Energy difference: ΔE = hν

ΔE = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 4.74 × 10¹⁴

Answer: ΔE ≈ 3.14 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Numerical 5: Photons Emitted in a Time Interval

Question: A 10 mW laser operates at a wavelength of 500 nm for 2 minutes. Calculate the total number of photons emitted, assuming constant power.

Given:

P = 10 mW = 0.01 W

t = 2 minutes = 120 s

λ = 500 × 10⁻⁹ m

Formula: N = Ptλ / hc

N = (0.01 × 120 × 500 × 10⁻⁹) / (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸)

Answer: N ≈ 3.02 × 10¹⁹ photons

1.02 Fibre Optics

1. Principle of Optical Fibre

An optical fibre is a thin, flexible transparent fibre used to transmit light signals.

It works on the principle of total internal reflection.

2. Structure of an Optical Fibre

  • Core: The central region through which light travels.
  • Cladding: The layer surrounding the core.
  • Protective coating: Protects the fibre from damage.

For total internal reflection, the refractive index of the core must be greater than that of the cladding.

3. Types of Optical Fibre

Step-Index Fibre

The refractive index changes suddenly at the boundary between the core and the cladding.

Graded-Index Fibre

The refractive index of the core gradually decreases from the centre towards the cladding.

Single-Mode Fibre

Single-mode fibre supports one principal propagation mode and is useful for long-distance communication.

Multimode Fibre

Multimode fibre supports multiple propagation modes.

4. Numerical Aperture

Numerical aperture describes the light-gathering ability of an optical fibre.

NA = n₀ sin θa

For a step-index fibre in air: NA = √(n₁² - n₂²)

  • n₁ = refractive index of the core
  • n₂ = refractive index of the cladding
  • n₀ = refractive index of the outside medium
  • θa = acceptance angle

5. Acceptance Angle

The acceptance angle is the maximum angle, measured relative to the fibre axis in the external medium, at which light can enter the fibre and still be guided.

In general: NA = n₀ sin θa

6. Losses in Optical Fibre

  • Absorption loss: Some optical energy is absorbed by the fibre material.
  • Scattering loss: Light is scattered due to microscopic variations in the material.
  • Bending loss: Light escapes when the fibre is bent too sharply.

7. Applications of Optical Fibre

  • Internet and telecommunications
  • Medical endoscopy
  • Industrial inspection
  • Optical sensors

8. Numerical Formula

Numerical aperture:

NA = √(n₁² - n₂²)

Acceptance angle in air:

θa = sin⁻¹(NA)

These simplified relations apply to a step-index fibre with air outside.

Solved Numerical

Question: The refractive index of the core is 1.5 and the refractive index of the cladding is 1.4. Calculate the numerical aperture (NA).

Formula: NA = √(n₁² − n₂²)

Given:

Core refractive index, n₁ = 1.5

Cladding refractive index, n₂ = 1.4

Solution:

NA = √(1.5² − 1.4²)

NA = √(2.25 − 1.96)

NA = √0.29

Answer: NA ≈ 0.5385

1.03 Holography

1. Definition

Holography is a technique used to record and reconstruct the wavefront of light from an object, producing a three-dimensional image effect.

2. Principle of Holography

Holography uses interference and diffraction of light to record and reconstruct information about an object's light wave.

3. Recording a Hologram

  1. Coherent laser light is split into two beams.
  2. The object beam illuminates the object.
  3. Light from the object reaches the recording plate.
  4. The reference beam also reaches the recording plate.
  5. The two beams form an interference pattern that is recorded.

4. Viewing a Hologram

When the recorded hologram is illuminated appropriately, it diffracts light and reconstructs the wavefront, creating a three-dimensional image effect.

5. Applications of Holography

  • Security labels and authentication
  • Scientific imaging
  • Data storage research
  • Three-dimensional displays
