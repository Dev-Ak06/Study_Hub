2.01 Crystal Structure

Crystal structure is the arrangement of atoms, ions or molecules in a solid. It describes how the constituent particles are arranged in space.

1. Structure of Solids

Solids are substances in which particles are closely packed and have definite volume. Based on the arrangement of their particles, solids are broadly classified into crystalline and amorphous solids.

1.1 Crystalline Solids

Definition: Crystalline solids are solids in which the constituent particles are arranged in a regular, repeating pattern over long distances. This is called long-range order.

Examples: Sodium chloride, quartz, diamond and copper.

Properties:

  • Long-range periodic arrangement.
  • Usually have a definite melting point.
  • Often show anisotropy, meaning certain physical properties depend on direction.
  • Have a characteristic crystal structure.

1.2 Amorphous Solids

Definition: Amorphous solids are solids that lack long-range periodic arrangement of their constituent particles. They may possess short-range order.

Examples: Glass, common plastics and many types of rubber.

Properties:

  • No long-range periodic arrangement.
  • Generally soften over a range of temperatures.
  • Usually show approximately isotropic properties.
  • Do not have the regular long-range crystal lattice of crystalline solids.

1.3 Difference Between Crystalline and Amorphous Solids

Property Crystalline Solid Amorphous Solid
Arrangement Long-range periodic order No long-range periodic order
Melting behaviour Usually melts at a definite temperature Generally softens over a temperature range
Directional properties May be anisotropic Usually approximately isotropic
Examples NaCl, copper, diamond Glass, plastic, rubber

2. Lattice, Basis and Unit Cell

2.1 Lattice

Definition: A space lattice is an infinite, periodic arrangement of points in three-dimensional space. Every lattice point has an equivalent environment.

Lattice points represent positions to which a basis is attached. They do not necessarily represent individual atoms.

2.2 Basis

Definition: A basis is an atom or a group of atoms associated with every lattice point.

Crystal structure = Space lattice + Basis

The same lattice can produce different crystal structures when different bases are attached to it.

2.3 Unit Cell

Definition: A unit cell is a repeating volume which, when translated through the lattice, reproduces the crystal structure.

The unit cell is described by six lattice parameters:

  • Edge lengths: a, b and c
  • Angles: alpha, beta and gamma

Volume of a cubic unit cell:

V = a3

Here, a is the edge length of the cube.

3. Fundamental Types of Lattices - Bravais Lattices

A Bravais lattice is a lattice in which every lattice point has an identical environment. There are 14 Bravais lattices grouped into seven crystal systems.

Types of Unit Cells

  • Primitive (P): Lattice points occur only at the corners.
  • Body-centred (I): Corner points plus one lattice point at the body centre.
  • Face-centred (F): Corner points plus lattice points at all six face centres.
  • Base-centred (C): Corner points plus lattice points at the centres of two opposite faces.

Seven Crystal Systems and Their Bravais Lattices

Crystal System Edge and Angle Conditions Bravais Lattices
Cubic a = b = c; all angles 90 degrees P, I, F
Tetragonal a = b != c; all angles 90 degrees P, I
Orthorhombic a != b != c; all angles 90 degrees P, C, I, F
Monoclinic a != b != c; two angles 90 degrees P, C
Triclinic a != b != c; angles are generally unequal and non-right P
Hexagonal a = b != c; alpha = beta = 90 degrees, gamma = 120 degrees P
Trigonal / Rhombohedral a = b = c; alpha = beta = gamma != 90 degrees R

Total number of Bravais lattices = 14.

Counts: Cubic 3, tetragonal 2, orthorhombic 4, monoclinic 2, triclinic 1, hexagonal 1 and rhombohedral 1.

4. Simple Cubic (SC) Lattice

In a simple cubic unit cell, lattice points occur at the eight corners of a cube.

4.1 Number of Atoms per Unit Cell

Each corner atom is shared by eight adjacent unit cells. Therefore, the contribution of one corner atom is 1/8.

N = 8 x 1/8

Formula 1: N = 1 atom per unit cell

4.2 Relation Between Atomic Radius and Lattice Constant

In a simple cubic structure, neighbouring corner atoms touch along the cube edge.

a = r + r

Formula 2: a = 2r

Therefore: r = a/2

4.3 Coordination Number

Each atom has six nearest neighbours: four in one plane, one above and one below.

Coordination number of SC = 6

4.4 Atomic Packing Factor

Atomic packing factor is the fraction of unit-cell volume occupied by the atoms, modelled as hard spheres.

APF = Volume of atoms / Volume of unit cell

APF = N x (4/3) pi r3 / a3

For SC, N = 1 and a = 2r.

APF = (4/3) pi r3 / (2r)3

APF = (4/3) pi r3 / 8r3

Formula 3: APF = pi/6 = 0.524 approximately

Packing percentage = 52.4%

5. Body-Centred Cubic (BCC) Lattice

In a BCC unit cell, lattice points occur at eight corners and one body centre.

5.1 Number of Atoms per Unit Cell

N = 8 x 1/8 + 1

Formula 4: N = 2 atoms per unit cell

5.2 Relation Between Atomic Radius and Lattice Constant

In BCC, atoms touch along the body diagonal. The body diagonal of a cube is found using Pythagoras' theorem.

Body diagonal = sqrt(a2 + a2 + a2)

Body diagonal = sqrt(3)a

Along the body diagonal, the touching atoms span four radii.

sqrt(3)a = 4r

Formula 5: a = 4r/sqrt(3)

Equivalent form: r = sqrt(3)a/4

5.3 Coordination Number

Each atom has eight nearest neighbours.

Coordination number of BCC = 8

5.4 Atomic Packing Factor Derivation

APF = N x (4/3) pi r3 / a3

For BCC, N = 2 and a = 4r/sqrt(3).

APF = 2 x (4/3) pi r3 / (4r/sqrt(3))3

APF = (8/3) pi r3 / (64r3/(3sqrt(3)))

Formula 6: APF = sqrt(3)pi/8 = 0.680 approximately

Packing percentage = 68.0%

6. Face-Centred Cubic (FCC) Lattice

In an FCC unit cell, lattice points occur at eight corners and at the centres of all six faces.

6.1 Number of Atoms per Unit Cell

N = 8 x 1/8 + 6 x 1/2

Formula 7: N = 4 atoms per unit cell

6.2 Relation Between Atomic Radius and Lattice Constant

In FCC, atoms touch along the face diagonal. The face diagonal is sqrt(2)a.

Face diagonal = sqrt(a2 + a2)

Face diagonal = sqrt(2)a

The touching atoms along this diagonal span four radii.

sqrt(2)a = 4r

Formula 8: a = 2sqrt(2)r

Equivalent form: r = a/(2sqrt(2))

6.3 Coordination Number

Each atom has twelve nearest neighbours.

Coordination number of FCC = 12

6.4 Atomic Packing Factor Derivation

APF = N x (4/3) pi r3 / a3

For FCC, N = 4 and a = 2sqrt(2)r.

APF = 4 x (4/3) pi r3 / (2sqrt(2)r)3

APF = (16/3) pi r3 / (16sqrt(2)r3)

Formula 9: APF = pi/(3sqrt(2)) = 0.740 approximately

Packing percentage = 74.0%

7. Miller Indices and Miller Planes

Miller indices are three integers (hkl) used to specify the orientation of a crystal plane.

7.1 Steps to Find Miller Indices

  1. Determine the intercepts of the plane on the x, y and z axes in units of a, b and c.
  2. Take the reciprocals of the intercepts.
  3. Multiply by a common factor if needed to obtain the smallest integer indices.
  4. Write the indices in round brackets as (hkl).

7.2 Example

Intercepts: a, 2b, infinity.

Intercepts in lattice units: 1, 2, infinity.

Reciprocals: 1, 1/2, 0.

Multiply by 2 to clear the fraction.

Miller indices = (210)

7.3 Important Rules

  • A plane parallel to an axis has an infinite intercept; its reciprocal is zero.
  • A negative index is represented by a bar over the index, for example (h-bar, k, l).
  • Indices are reduced to the smallest whole-number ratio.
  • Plane indices are written as (hkl); a family of equivalent planes is written as {hkl}.

7.4 Interplanar Spacing in a Cubic Crystal

For cubic crystals, the perpendicular spacing between adjacent planes of the (hkl) family is:

Formula 10: d(hkl) = a/sqrt(h2 + k2 + l2)

This formula applies to cubic crystals. Other crystal systems have different interplanar-spacing expressions.

8. Bragg's Equation

Bragg's equation describes the condition for constructive interference of X-rays scattered by parallel crystal planes.

Formula 11: n lambda = 2d sin(theta)

Where:

  • n = order of diffraction (positive integer)
  • lambda = wavelength of X-rays
  • d = distance between adjacent crystal planes
  • theta = Bragg angle, measured between the incident beam and the crystal plane

8.1 Derivation of Bragg's Equation

Consider two parallel X-ray beams reflected from adjacent crystal planes separated by distance d.

The lower beam travels an extra distance on entering and leaving the deeper plane.

Extra path length = d sin(theta) + d sin(theta)

Extra path length = 2d sin(theta)

For constructive interference, the path difference must be an integer multiple of the wavelength.

Path difference = n lambda

Therefore:

n lambda = 2d sin(theta)

Hence proved.

8.2 Rearranged Forms

Formula 12: d = n lambda/[2 sin(theta)]

Formula 13: lambda = 2d sin(theta)/n

Formula 14: theta = sin-1[n lambda/(2d)]

8.3 Conditions

  • The path difference must be n lambda.
  • For a physical diffraction angle, n lambda/(2d) must not exceed 1.
  • Wavelength and interplanar spacing must use the same units.

9. Applications of Bragg's Equation and Crystal Structure

  • Crystal structure determination: X-ray diffraction helps determine atomic arrangements in crystals.
  • Lattice-spacing measurement: Bragg diffraction can be used to determine distances between crystal planes.
  • Material identification: Diffraction patterns help identify crystalline materials and phases.
  • Semiconductor research: Crystal analysis is useful for studying semiconductor materials.
  • Material quality analysis: X-ray diffraction can reveal structural changes, strain and defects in suitable measurements.
  • Scientific research: Crystal structures of minerals, metals, ceramics and other materials can be studied.

10. Important Formula Summary

  1. Cubic unit-cell volume: V = a3
  2. SC atoms per unit cell: N = 1
  3. BCC atoms per unit cell: N = 2
  4. FCC atoms per unit cell: N = 4
  5. SC radius relation: a = 2r
  6. BCC radius relation: sqrt(3)a = 4r
  7. FCC radius relation: sqrt(2)a = 4r
  8. General APF: N(4/3)pi r3/a3
  9. SC APF: pi/6 = 0.524 approximately
  10. BCC APF: sqrt(3)pi/8 = 0.680 approximately
  11. FCC APF: pi/(3sqrt(2)) = 0.740 approximately
  12. Cubic interplanar spacing: d = a/sqrt(h2+k2+l2)
  13. Bragg's equation: n lambda = 2d sin(theta)
  14. Density: rho = NM/(NAa3)

In the density formula, N is the number of atoms per unit cell, M is molar mass, NA is Avogadro's constant and a is the cubic lattice constant. Use consistent units.

11. Ten Solved Numerical Examples

Numerical 1: Number of Atoms in BCC

Question: Calculate the number of atoms in a BCC unit cell.

Formula: N = 8(1/8) + 1

N = 1 + 1

Answer: N = 2 atoms

Numerical 2: Number of Atoms in FCC

Question: Calculate the number of atoms in an FCC unit cell.

Formula: N = 8(1/8) + 6(1/2)

N = 1 + 3

Answer: N = 4 atoms

Numerical 3: Atomic Radius of Simple Cubic

Question: The edge length of a simple cubic unit cell is 4 Angstrom. Calculate its atomic radius.

Formula: r = a/2

r = 4/2

Answer: r = 2 Angstrom

Numerical 4: Atomic Radius of BCC

Question: The lattice constant of a BCC crystal is 3.0 Angstrom. Calculate its atomic radius.

Formula: r = sqrt(3)a/4

r = sqrt(3) x 3.0/4

r = 1.299 Angstrom approximately

Answer: r ≈ 1.30 Angstrom

Numerical 5: Atomic Radius of FCC

Question: An FCC crystal has lattice constant 4 Angstrom. Calculate the atomic radius.

Formula: r = a/(2sqrt(2))

r = 4/(2 x 1.4142)

Answer: r ≈ 1.414 Angstrom

Numerical 6: APF of Simple Cubic

Question: Calculate the atomic packing factor of SC.

Formula: APF = pi/6

APF = 3.1416/6

Answer: APF ≈ 0.524 or 52.4%

Numerical 7: Interplanar Spacing

Question: A cubic crystal has lattice constant a = 4 Angstrom. Calculate the interplanar spacing for (110).

Formula: d = a/sqrt(h2 + k2 + l2)

d = 4/sqrt(12 + 12 + 02)

d = 4/sqrt(2)

Answer: d ≈ 2.828 Angstrom

Numerical 8: Bragg Angle

Question: X-rays of wavelength 1.54 Angstrom are reflected from planes separated by 2 Angstrom. Calculate the first-order Bragg angle.

Formula: n lambda = 2d sin(theta)

sin(theta) = 1 x 1.54/(2 x 2)

sin(theta) = 0.385

theta = sin-1(0.385)

Answer: theta ≈ 22.6 degrees

Numerical 9: Interplanar Spacing from Bragg's Equation

Question: X-rays of wavelength 1.5 Angstrom give first-order diffraction at a Bragg angle of 30 degrees. Calculate d.

Formula: d = n lambda/[2 sin(theta)]

d = 1 x 1.5/[2 x sin(30 degrees)]

d = 1.5/(2 x 0.5)

Answer: d = 1.5 Angstrom

Numerical 10: Bragg's Equation for Second Order

Question: X-rays of wavelength 1 Angstrom are incident on crystal planes separated by 2 Angstrom. Calculate the second-order Bragg angle.

Given: lambda = 1 Angstrom, d = 2 Angstrom, n = 2

Formula: sin(theta) = n lambda/(2d)

sin(theta) = (2 x 1)/(2 x 2)

sin(theta) = 0.5

theta = sin-1(0.5)

Answer: theta = 30 degrees
